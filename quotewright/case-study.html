Product case study

Quotewright: from scattered spreadsheets to a priced quote in one place

A cost estimating and quoting tool for small project-based businesses in construction, installation and logistics. I defined the problem, set success metrics, prioritised the scope and built a working prototype.

  • RoleProduct owner, designer, developer
  • TypeWeb app prototype
  • StackHTML, CSS, JavaScript
  • ScopeMVP, 1 release

The problem

In project work I prepared many cost estimates and quotes. In small companies the process usually looks the same: costs live in a personal spreadsheet, margins are added by hand, and the client quote is retyped into a Word template.

That process creates three recurring problems:

  1. Inconsistent pricing. Overhead and contingency depend on who prepares the quote, so two estimators price the same job differently.
  2. Margin confusion. "20% on top" (markup) and "20% margin" are mixed up. On a €100,000 job that gap is worth over €3,000 of profit.
  3. Slow, error-prone quoting. Copying numbers from the spreadsheet into the quote document takes time and introduces mistakes.
Problem statement: Estimators at small project-based businesses need a consistent way to turn direct costs into a correctly priced, client-ready quote, because today's manual process causes underpricing, slow turnaround and errors.

Key decisions and trade-offs

1. Margin on price, not markup on cost

Owners think in margin, but most spreadsheets add markup. Quotewright asks for target margin and shows the equivalent markup next to it, so both views are visible and the confusion disappears.

2. The client never sees the internal cost sheet

The quote groups costs by category and spreads overhead, contingency and profit across them. The client sees scope and price; the company keeps its cost structure private.

3. No backend in version 1

Data is saved in the browser. That limits sharing, but it let me test the workflow with zero hosting cost and zero setup for users. Accounts come only after the workflow is validated.

4. Warnings, not blocks

A quote below 12% margin shows a warning instead of being blocked. Estimators sometimes have good reasons to price low; the tool's job is to make that a conscious decision.
